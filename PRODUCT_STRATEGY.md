# Product Strategy: Finding PMF with Three MVPs

## The Three-Product Experiment

### Why This Strategy Works (Inspired by Your Heroes):

**Steve Jobs**: "Focus is about saying no to the hundred other good ideas"
- By running 3 MVPs, you'll quickly learn which ONE to focus on

**Elon Musk**: "Fast iteration beats slow perfection"  
- Ship all three, see what sticks, iterate rapidly

**Jack Dorsey**: "Make every detail perfect and limit the number of details"
- Each MVP is simple, focused on one core interaction

## Should Truth Market Questions Feed Into Headlines?

### My Recommendation: NO (At First)

**Keep them separate because:**

1. **Different User Behaviors**
   - Truth Market: Thoughtful, open-ended exploration
   - Headlines: Quick, daily habit, entertainment

2. **Different Quality Bars**
   - Headlines need curated, timely, resolvable predictions
   - Truth Market allows any question (quality varies)

3. **Different Resolution Times**
   - Headlines: 24-hour resolution
   - Truth Market: Variable (hours to years)

### Future Integration (After PMF):

If Headlines wins, you could:
1. Let high-stakes Truth Market questions "graduate" to Headlines
2. Use Headlines as daily "featured predictions" from Truth Market
3. Create "Headlines Pro" with user-generated predictions

## The Real Insight: You're Testing Three Business Models

1. **Ask Page** → B2B SaaS (API for multi-LLM consensus)
2. **Truth Market** → Prediction Market Platform (Polymarket competitor)  
3. **Headlines** → Consumer App (Wordle for predictions)

## My Bet on Which Wins:

**Headlines has the best shot at PMF because:**
- Lowest friction (swipe vs type)
- Daily habit potential  
- Clear value prop (entertainment + earn)
- Viral mechanics built-in (streaks)

**Truth Market is the best long-term business:**
- Deeper engagement
- Natural monetization (take fees)
- Network effects (more users = better predictions)
- Platform potential

**Ask Page is the easiest to monetize:**
- Charge for API access
- Enterprise deals
- Clear B2B value prop

## The Dorsey Approach to Code:

Your codebase should be:
1. **Modular** - Each MVP can be deleted without breaking others
2. **Minimal** - No feature unless users beg for it  
3. **Beautiful** - The code you keep should be poetry

## The Meta Strategy:

You're not just building products - you're building a learning machine. Each MVP teaches you something different about the market. The fastest learner wins.

Remember Dorsey's Twitter: Started as podcasting platform, pivoted to SMS status updates. The pivot only happened because they shipped and learned.

Ship all three. Let users vote with their time. Then focus ruthlessly on the winner.
